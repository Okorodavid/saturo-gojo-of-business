## Questions, care and economic claims

PDF347–349's insurance-adjuster example starts with checking whether everyone escaped a fire safely, then explores prior claims, representation, administrative burden and negotiating confidence. The humane opening and the operational burden are concrete. Later leading questions assume a representation choice follows from the need for help. The claimed30%settlement improvement and10%fee require the fee base to be stated: if a$1,000initial offer becomes$1,300and the fee is10%of that total, the client receives$1,170, not$1,200. If the fee uses another base, the result differs. An average improvement and a repair-cost fee guarantee do not establish that every prospect has no downside. These are historical sales claims, not current insurance or legal guidance.

The question-library advice on349–351 recommends collecting, adapting and rehearsing language. The religious examples are the author's beliefs and analogies, not instructions to the reader or proof of selling effectiveness. The dinner story shows framing an alternative in the listener's desired outcome. It cannot establish that every presented alternative is actually in a buyer's interest.

PDF357–358 uses an attention puzzle to illustrate retaining the objective. Its inference that topic changes intentionally divert a seller is only a hypothesis; a person may have another urgent concern or want the interaction to end. Clear focus need not become dismissal of what was said.

## Persistence contains its own boundaries

PDF359 explicitly rejects repeated sign-here demands as pressure. The encyclopedia story360 concerns several hours needed to communicate across a language barrier, not evidence that all hesitant people should face a four-hour presentation. PDF361–363 says pressure depends on how the particular prospect feels, and363's example promises to leave immediately if asked. These qualifications must constrain the belief-based persistence rhetoric and the statement about whose will weakens first.

A truthful competing offer on a home can be material information; fabricating it would be different. The dental-crown case362–363 contains unverified clinical reasoning and assumes that coverage supports completing more treatment. Insurance payment alone does not establish clinical necessity. Preserve the general point about explaining a professional recommendation clearly without treating this sales anecdote as medical guidance.

## Service can matter more than winning the immediate order

The Doyle clothier narrative364–367 links finding a requested item, timely tailoring, relevant selection, checking satisfaction and reducing the buyer's effort. The phrase about fair treatment explicitly allows the seller a profit. Trust-based price reassurance is said to be earned over years, not a substitute for transparent terms with a stranger. Reported referrals and large cumulative sales are anecdotes, not predictable returns on every small favor.

The bicycle example368–369 is a decisive counterweight to pressure techniques: the owner refuses to sell a bicycle too large for the child despite the grandmother's insistence. The praised outcome is losing that unsafe sale while preserving integrity. It shows the book is not coherently read as conversion at any cost.

The Signature Close369–372 is explicitly limited to certain items and a late stage after value has been established. It connects a signature with marriage, children, investments and personal progress, then uses silence. These analogies do not show that this particular purchase or recruitment opportunity is wise. A real need to consult a partner or advisor remains a valid decision condition. The child-and-ball narrative373–375 illustrates persuasion through subordinate requests, repeated action and an inducement; obtaining a thirteenth ball does not prove need or superior buyer welfare.

## Harrington adds business-building and funding

PDF376 onward is Kevin Harrington's added contribution and must not all be attributed to Zig. It distinguishes selling competence from operating and scaling a business, and acknowledges that not every salesperson should become an entrepreneur or hire full-time staff. Fractional help can match limited needs. Advice from mentors and experts is balanced on379by people close to the product, team and customer whose feedback can expose failure.

The ten-step investor presentation380–386 covers problem, solution, demonstration, additional value, testimonial types, research, team, use of funds, distribution and a clear ask. Consumer experience, expert opinion, laboratory evidence, editorial coverage and celebrity influence are not equally strong proof. Status, quantity of data or a convincing demonstration does not eliminate uncertainty or establish unique superiority.

The accelerated-payback example adds a per-unit payment until capital is returned and equity afterward. This rearranges cash flow and risk; it is not free money, guaranteed funding or automatically favorable to the founder. Equity does not simply entitle an investor to a fixed percentage of revenue. Actual rights and obligations depend on terms not specified by the anecdote.

The nine-part plan386–390 includes summary, market, competitors, sales/distribution, proof, operations, people, projections and risks/exit. Most useful is the explicit question of actual sales and willingness to pay. The$5,000sales from$2,000advertising example is evidence of purchases, not by itself profitable repeatable acquisition; delivery costs, refunds and collection timing are absent. Forecasts need assumptions and evidence, not a hope placed between two arbitrary revenue endpoints. Claims that a plan automatically places someone in the top10%are unsupported here.

Partnership examples390–392 stress access, reputation, capabilities and the other party's goals. Supplier exclusivity exchanged for financing carries opportunity costs and obligations. A reported bank-credit success after adding a team is one case, not proof that presentation alone creates creditworthiness. The1947first-sale story392–393 includes returning when the prospect's spouse could attend, qualifying any supposed universal rule against consultations or callbacks.
