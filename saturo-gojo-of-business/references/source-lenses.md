# Source lenses

Use the smallest relevant combination. These are interpretations, not endorsements of every claim or technique in each source.

## Brian Tracy — closing mechanics

Useful for confidence, objection categories, readiness signals, and ways to ask for a decision. Filter out presumptive pressure, unsupported psychology, and any technique that overrides discovery or a clear refusal.

Detailed notes: `close-reading/part-001.md`.

## Art Sobczak — smart prospecting

The strongest prospecting discipline in the pack: prepare from observable facts, form a *possible* value hypothesis, earn attention, explore, listen, and secure an appropriate next action. Prefer this lens for cold calls and first conversations.

Detailed notes: `close-reading/part-002.md` and `part-003.md`.

## Mike Brooks — phone scripts and practice

Useful for reusable language, objection drills, qualification, and call structure. Scripts are scaffolding; adapt them to the buyer and never use them to conceal, pressure, or impersonate.

Detailed notes: `close-reading/part-004.md` through `part-006.md`.

## Daniel Pink — attunement, clarity, and service

Useful for perspective-taking, resilience, problem finding, communication, and service orientation. Treat social-science anecdotes and universal causal claims cautiously.

Detailed notes: `close-reading/part-007.md` and `part-008.md`.

## Zig Ziglar — stories and classic closes

Useful for confidence, explanation, objection handling, and memorable teaching examples. Many examples are dated or stereotyped; preserve autonomy and update the language.

Detailed notes: `close-reading/part-009.md` through `part-012.md`.

## Anthony Iannarino — buyer commitments

Best for complex deals that stall. Model progress through commitments such as time, exploration, change, collaboration, consensus, investment, review, concerns, decision, and execution.

Detailed notes: `close-reading/part-013.md` and `part-014.md`.

## Miller and Heiman — strategic selling

Use for multi-stakeholder opportunity diagnosis, red flags, ideal-customer fit, and economic/user/technical/coach roles. Avoid adding this machinery to a simple sale.

Detailed notes: `close-reading/part-015.md` through `part-017.md`.

## Challenger — commercial insight

Useful when the seller has evidence that genuinely changes how a buyer sees a costly problem. Tailor the insight and guide the process. Do not reduce the approach to provocation, certainty, or aggressive control.

Detailed notes: `close-reading/part-018.md` and `part-019.md`.

## Alex Hormozi books — offers, leads, and money models

Strong for offer decomposition, value, lead-channel structure, contribution economics, cash timing, retention, and constraint thinking. Numerical rules, guarantees, examples, and self-reported results require independent checking.

Detailed notes: `close-reading/part-020.md` through `part-024.md`.

## Alex Hormozi transcripts — tactical cases and operating patterns

Useful as a large case library for offers, sales calls, pricing, content, hiring, retention, and constraints. Treat repeated promotional claims, dramatic numbers, legal or tax generalizations, platform tactics, and causal stories as unverified unless checked.

YouTube notes: `close-reading/part-025.md` through `part-056.md`.

Older short-form transcript notes: `close-reading/part-057.md` through `part-060.md`.
